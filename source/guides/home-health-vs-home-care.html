Home health vs. home care: the distinction that saves a wasted week

Home health is skilled, usually Medicare-certified care. Home care is help with daily life. Families search the wrong list when they mix them up.

HomeAide editors · Updated October 2, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Home health agencies in this directory are Medicare-certified and report quality measures to CMS.
  • Home care / private duty is a different market: hourly help, often without a skilled-need requirement.
  • You may need both at once — a nurse twice a week and an aide for evenings.

Two industries, one kitchen table

Discharge planners say “we’ll set up home health.” Relatives hear “someone will be there.” Then a nurse visits for 45 minutes and leaves. That gap is the difference between home health and home care.

Home health

Skilled services ordered by a clinician, delivered by a Medicare-certified agency, with start-of-care assessments, quality reporting, and a defined episode. This is what HomeAide indexes.

Home care

Personal care, meals, rides, companionship. Payment is private, sometimes long-term care insurance or Medicaid. CMS star ratings on this site do not describe that workforce.
