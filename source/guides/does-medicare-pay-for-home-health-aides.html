Does Medicare pay for a home health aide?

Medicare can pay for home health aide visits — but only as part of a skilled home health plan, not as all-day companion care.

HomeAide editors · Updated October 2, 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways

  • Medicare Part A or B may cover intermittent home health aide services when you also need skilled nursing or therapy.
  • Aide hours are usually brief visits, not 24-hour private duty.
  • Use the HomeAide directory to find agencies that list home health aide services, then confirm with the agency and your clinician.

The short answer families actually need

Original Medicare can cover home health aide help with bathing, dressing, and light personal care when those visits are part of a Medicare-certified home health episode. The episode has to include a skilled need — nursing, physical therapy, speech, or a continuing occupational therapy plan. An aide-only schedule for companionship or housekeeping is generally not a Medicare home health benefit.

What “intermittent” means in real life

Coverage is built around part-time or intermittent care, typically visits rather than live-in shifts. If your parent needs someone in the house overnight, that is usually private-duty home care, Medicaid waiver services, long-term care insurance, or family — not the Medicare home health aide line item.
