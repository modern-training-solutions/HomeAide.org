A simple plan-of-care checklist for the first week at home

Agencies work from a clinical plan of care. Families still need a household plan so the aide and the daughter are not improvising.

HomeAide editors · Updated October 2, 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Put meds, allergies, and the 24-hour contact on one sheet on the fridge.
  • Separate clinical tasks from household tasks so nobody assumes the nurse will do laundry.
  • Review the plan after three visits — needs change fast.

On the fridge

Primary doctor, pharmacy, agency after-hours line, DNR/POLST status if it exists, mobility limits, and “do not” items (no leftover rugs, no left-side blood pressure, etc.).

Daily rhythm

Wake time, meals, toileting help, planned walks, and the therapy homework. Home health works better when the visit is not a surprise in the middle of a nap.
