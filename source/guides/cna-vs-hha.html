CNA vs. home health aide: who might walk through your door

Titles vary by state. What matters is training, what the agency is certified to send, and what Medicare will pay for on that visit.

HomeAide editors · Updated October 2, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • A home health aide on a Medicare plan is there for personal care tied to the skilled plan — not a full private-duty shift.
  • CNA credentials are state-specific; ask the agency what they require.
  • If you need hours of household help, budget for home care in addition to home health.

Ask one clarifying sentence

“Who is coming, what license or competency do they hold, and which tasks are they allowed to do in this state?” Then match that answer to the services flags on the agency’s HomeAide profile.
